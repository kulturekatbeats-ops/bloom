The fourth trimester, tracked gently

The weeks after birth are their own season — recovery, feeding, almost no sleep, and a flood of feeling. A little structure helps; pressure doesn't.

What's worth tracking after birth

Follow your provider's instructions first, especially after a C-section or complicated birth. Beyond that, gentle tracking of a few things can help you spot patterns and answer the inevitable "when did that last happen?":

  • Your recovery — bleeding, pain, stitches or incision healing, first appointments.
  • Mood and sleep — a private, honest check-in matters more now than ever.
  • Feeding — breast or bottle, times and sides, so you're not counting on memory at 3am.
  • Baby basics — diapers, sleep, and anything your pediatrician asked you to watch.

Postpartum warning signs

Your body is doing major healing. Contact your provider or emergency services right away for:

  • Heavy bleeding — soaking a pad an hour, or large clots
  • Severe headache, vision changes, or chest pain / trouble breathing
  • Fever, or a hot, painful, red area on a breast or leg
  • Signs of infection around an incision or stitches
  • Thoughts of harming yourself or your baby
If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, this is an emergency — contact your provider or emergency services immediately. You deserve help, and it's available. General information only, not medical advice.

Mind as well as body

The "baby blues" — tearfulness and mood swings — are common in the first couple of weeks. If low mood, anxiety, hopelessness, or trouble bonding lingers past two weeks or feels overwhelming, that may be postpartum depression or anxiety. Both are common and treatable. Telling your provider isn't a failure; it's good parenting.

Common questions

What should I track postpartum?
Your recovery (bleeding, pain, healing), mood and sleep, feeding and diapers, and baby basics — guided by your provider.
What are postpartum warning signs?
Heavy bleeding, severe headache, vision changes, chest pain, fever, a hot painful breast or leg, or thoughts of harm. Seek urgent care.
How do I know if it's postpartum depression?
Baby blues are common for two weeks. If low mood or anxiety lasts longer or feels overwhelming, talk to your provider — it's common and treatable.
