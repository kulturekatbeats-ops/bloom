How to count baby kicks

Counting movements is one of the simplest, most reassuring things you can do — and a real change from your baby's normal is information your care team wants quickly.

Step by step

  1. Pick a regular time. Babies have active periods, often after you eat. Counting around the same time each day helps a pattern emerge.
  2. Get comfortable. Sit with your feet up, or lie on your side — many people find their left side easiest later in pregnancy.
  3. Count to 10. Start a timer and count any kick, roll, or flutter. Note how long 10 movements takes. For many people during an active spell it's well under two hours.
  4. Learn your baby's normal. Over a few days you'll see a typical timeframe. The number isn't the point — your baby's own pattern is.
  5. Act on changes. Slower, stopped, or different movement than usual means call your maternity unit now.
Reduced movement can be an important sign. Contact your maternity unit straight away if your baby's movements change — day or night. Don't use a home doppler for reassurance. General information, not medical advice.

Common questions

When should I start counting kicks?
Often from around 28 weeks, though you'll feel movement earlier. Follow your provider's advice, especially if high-risk.
How many kicks are normal?
There's no magic number — your baby's own pattern matters. Many note how long 10 movements takes during an active period.
What if my baby isn't moving?
If movements slow, stop, or change, contact your maternity unit immediately, any hour. Don't wait, and don't rely on home dopplers.
